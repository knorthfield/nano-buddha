## Description (4000)
Nano Buddha is a meditation timer for people who check the clock. The sit screen shows no time at all. A singing bowl tells you when your time has passed, and you sit on until you are ready to end.

HOW IT WORKS
• Choose how long you sit the first time. After that, nudge it by a minute if you want.
• Tap Begin and put your phone down. After 30 seconds of silence, the bowl rings to open your sit.
• The bowl rings again when your time has passed. Sit on, or tap End when you are ready.
• The exact time changes a little each sit, up to a minute either way, so you cannot count it.
• Each sit you complete makes the next one 15 seconds longer. Your practice grows slowly, without effort.

ON ALL YOUR DEVICES
• iPhone and iPad, with a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island.
• Apple Watch: a gentle tap on the wrist for each bell, with the bowl sound if you want it. Complications and a Smart Stack widget.
• Apple TV: sit in front of a slowly turning galaxy.
• Mac: a window and desktop widgets.
• Your sits stay the same on all your devices through iCloud.

BEGIN A SIT YOUR WAY

• A Control Centre button.
• Siri: "Begin a sit in Nano Buddha".
• Shortcuts and the Action button.

SEE YOUR PRACTICE
• History of every sit, with your total time.
• Apple Health: each sit is saved as Mindful Minutes.
• Copy week puts a log of your last seven days on the clipboard.

CALM TO LOOK AT
A spiral galaxy on black in dark mode, grains of sand on old paper in light mode.

PRIVATE
Nano Buddha has no account, no ads and no tracking. Your data stays on your devices and in your own iCloud.

## Review notes
1. No login or setup is needed. The app has no account, no user-generated content and no paid content.

2. Purpose and audience: Nano Buddha is a meditation timer for people who meditate, from beginners to people with a daily practice. The problem: a visible timer makes people watch the clock instead of meditating. Nano Buddha shows no time while you sit. A singing bowl rings at the start and when the time has passed, and each completed sit makes the next one 15 seconds longer.

3. The sit screen intentionally shows no time and no countdown, and the app is intentionally silent for the first 30 seconds after Begin. This is the design, not a fault. To test:
   - Open the app. On the first run, pick a duration (for example 5 min) and tap Begin. Allow notifications: the bells also come as notifications when the phone is locked.
   - After 30 seconds the singing bowl rings to open the sit. It rings again when the time has passed (the time is the chosen duration plus or minus up to one minute).
   - Tap End at any time. The app asks for permission to save the sit to Apple Health as Mindful Minutes (optional), then shows the Done screen. The History button shows past sits.
   - A running sit shows a Live Activity with an End button on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island.
   - Apple Watch: the same flow; the bells are a wrist tap, and the bowl sound plays when the Sound toggle is on. Apple TV: the same flow; the Menu button ends the sit. Mac: the same flow; Escape ends the sit.

4. External services: none. The app makes no network requests of its own. It uses only Apple frameworks: HealthKit (optional, writes Mindful Minutes, reads nothing), the iCloud key-value store (syncs the user's sits between their own devices), WatchConnectivity, WidgetKit, ActivityKit, App Intents and UserNotifications.

5. Regional differences: none. The app works the same in all regions.

6. Regulated industry or third-party material: not applicable. Nano Buddha is not a medical app. The singing bowl sound is "Tibetan Bowl Struck #1" from BigSoundBank, released under CC0 (public domain).
